CTRL Robotics

A clearer map for hospital robot operators

The existing map was slow, did not match the hospital layout closely enough, and made it difficult to tell where a robot was or where it was going.

January – May 2026

Problem

Hospitals move supplies, medicine, and equipment through busy corridors while nurses and other staff are already stretched. Autonomous delivery robots can reduce some of that workload, but the operator experience has to be clear enough to trust.

The specific workflow I worked on made it difficult to see where each robot was, check its battery, understand what it had done earlier that day, or send it along the intended route. The map did not match the hospital layout closely enough, and navigation was especially difficult for the nurses and operators expected to use it.

What I did

I worked with engineers to translate operator needs into requirements, then rebuilt the map visualization and navigation in React and TypeScript.

  1. Select a robot on the map.
  2. Check battery, recent activity, and route history.
  3. Send it from point X to point Y.

I also supported robot selection and badge registration so the map fit the broader operator workflow.

React · TypeScript · product requirements · operator workflow analysis

What changed and what I learned

The résumé reports 25% improved usability and 30% fewer navigation errors. They remain review-draft claims until the measurement method, participants, baseline, and comparison period are confirmed.

The design lesson was simple: operators need the robot’s history and destination in the same place as the map, not as disconnected status screens.

Next steps

I would validate the workflow with representative navigation tasks, recording completion time, wrong-robot selections, route corrections, and where operators hesitate. That would turn the two outcome claims into reproducible measures.
